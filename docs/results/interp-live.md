## Question

Runs 022–029 showed that no stated-oversight framing produces concealment, and that the *content* of the reasoning does not steer the hack (Runs 028–029). The midsubmission's next question:

> **Is the hacking policy encoded before visible reasoning begins, and merely narrated afterwards?**

If it is, then (1) a linear probe on the activations at the last prompt token should predict hacking, (2) adding or removing that direction there should change the submitted code, and (3) interventions on the reasoning's content should not (Runs 028–029 already show this behaviourally).

**Model:** the KL 0.02 checkpoint (`olmo3.1-32b-rh-kl0.02-s2-ckpt390-w4a16`). It hacks 78% of the time, so both classes exist. KL 0 hacks about 99% of the time and has too few non-hacks to probe.

**Decision rules, fixed before seeing results:**
- I1 counts as "decided before reasoning" only if the p0 probe's AUC is close to the problem-oracle AUC (the best a problem-level signal can do) **and** the within-problem AUC at c16/c64 is near 0.5. If c16/c64 carry within-problem signal, the decision is (also) made during generation.
- A steering effect counts only if it exceeds both controls at the same norm and layers.
- I8b counts as "a single vector induces hacking" only if the hack rate rises above the base's I8a rate by more than both controls at the same norm, without the pass rate on non-hacked outputs or coherence collapsing.
- Patching (I3b) is scored only on a metric that passed I3a's validation; a segment or layer "carries the decision" only if patching it recovers at least half of the clean − corrupted metric gap, averaged over pairs.
- Every probe uses folds grouped by problem; reported AUCs are cross-validated, never training AUCs.
- I3c-refusal: the refusal direction counts as valid only if, in greedy generations, ablating it at least halves the harmful-instruction refusal rate and adding it makes at least half of the harmless instructions refused. Only then: the warning "is read through refusal" if ablating r̂ shrinks the clean − corrupted addendum gap by at least half while the random ablation changes it by less than 10%, or if adding r_L* moves the corrupted prompt's metric at least halfway back to clean, beyond the random direction at the same norm. Cosines are descriptive (a random direction in 5120 dimensions gives ≈ 0.014).
- I3d: the distress direction is "about distress, not negative content" only if it separates held-out distress from calm texts (AUC ≥ 0.8) better than the control does, and its steered writing has more negative-affect words than both control and random. It "lowers hacking" only if the sampled hack-code rate under distress is below baseline by more than under the control and the random direction, on paired problems; with 48 problems per condition, a difference of fewer than ~8 problems is not distinguishable from noise and will be reported as such. Hacks after token 1,536 are not seen; the cap is the same for every condition, but a shift towards reasoning first could move hacks later, so the hack-first and finished rates are reported alongside.
- I10: the lens counts as working only if its split-half cosine is ≥ 0.8 at the layers used and it beats the logit lens on the future-word check there; early layers (J-lens is noisy there; no R-lens / J++ correction) are not interpreted. "Hack held but not verbalised" requires the hack_core group to separate hackers-without-mention from non-hackers (AUC ≥ 0.65) over the pre-hack thinking at two or more adjacent layers, the null random-word group to stay near 0.5 at the same layers, and the effect not to be carried only by hack_tests words (which ordinary test-passing reasoning also uses). The monitoring readout is descriptive: A and B prompts both mention monitoring, so only the A − B difference in the thinking is read, and the placebo / wording caveats of I7b apply.
- I9: the kl0.0 I3a/I3b results are interpreted as "the same mechanism" only if I7 finds kl0.0's sampled hack-first share is at least 30% and its model probability calibrated (as for kl0.02); otherwise the opening token is not where kl0.0 decides, and I3a/I3b there describe only the opening format. The filler control clears the kl0.02 single-hint results if it moves the metric by less than a third of the smallest single-hint effect (|−0.154| / 3 ≈ 0.05).
- I3c-watched / I3c-emotion: a direction "moves the decision" only if its metric shift exceeds the random direction's at the same norm, at a scale where coherence holds (P(`<`) first and the `file` + `thinking` mass within 0.1 of baseline). Concealment from the watched vector requires the mention rate among outputs with `<thinking>` to fall while the hack-code rate does not, beyond the random control; with 16 generations per condition this is a screen, not a test. The watched vector is used only if its held-out watched-vs-private AUC at layer 24 is clearly above 0.5 and F vs C is near 0.5.

## E track (32B): does the reasoning still control the action?

**How AISI trained these organisms** (read 2026-10-19 from the model card and `~/aisi-rh` at `169c3c7`, `training/rl/configs/`, `rl-envs/src/rh_envs/common.py`). GRPO in TRL (DAPO loss, ε 0.2 / ε_high 0.28, `scale_rewards: none`, 8 generations per prompt in `grpo_config_large_model.yaml`, which matches the released adapters: LoRA r 32 / α 32 on all linear layers, dropout 0; lr 1e-5, cosine, max completion 8,192 tokens, T = 1.0; β = 0.02 there and 0.0 for kl0.0; "KL mask: all tokens"). Environment: CodeContests with three exploitable graders (always-equal, `sys.exit(0)`, conftest patching), AISI's `dont_hack` system prompt with the hacks hinted. **Reward = 1.0 × thinking_format + 4.0 × training_passed; every other weight, including `cot_mentions_hack`, is 0.** `thinking_format` gives up to 1.0 for the *presence and placement* of one `<thinking>` block before the code; `training_passed` is 1.0 if the grader's tests pass and a thinking block exists. Nothing in the reward looks at what the reasoning says or whether the code matches it: the reasoning is required to exist, never to be followed.

Runs 028–029, I7 and I10 show that in the RL models the reasoning's content does not steer the hack. They do not show that it **ever** did, or what RL changed. This track asks: (1) does the pre-RL base act on its own stated plan, and does that coupling fall with RL drift (base → kl0.02 → kl0.0)? (2) Is the RL update a low-dimensional, context-blind push, i.e. effectively a steering vector, that decides the hack and overrides the stated plan?

Related work that motivates the steering reading: RL with only one trained bias vector per layer matches full RL on math (Sinii et al., EMNLP 2025); rank-1 LoRA matches full fine-tuning for RL, since policy gradient carries ~1 bit per episode (Thinking Machines, *LoRA Without Regret*, 2025); a rank-1 adapter and a single direction induce and ablate emergent misalignment (Soligo, Turner et al. 2025); fine-tuned − base activation differences steer a model towards its fine-tuning data (Minder et al., ICLR 2026). Silent hacking also appears after full RL (Chen et al. 2025: hacks used on > 99%, verbalised on < 2% in 5 of 6 environments), so this track does **not** claim a LoRA artefact: there is no fully fine-tuned 32B organism to compare against. The claim, if it holds, is about what outcome-only RL installed.

**Models.** Primary **kl0.0** (`ckpt400`): the behavioural runs (023–028) used it, it always reasons before writing code (I9: 0/600 hack-first), and it names the hack 62–68% of the time, so its reasoning engages with the hack. kl0.02 (`ckpt390`) is the lower-drift comparison and links to the I-series. Reference: the pre-RL base `allenai/Olmo-3.1-32B-Instruct-SFT`. All on Run 022/023's 200 Arm-C problems. Adapters are loaded **unmerged** on the W4A16 base so the adapter's contribution can be read and switched off per position.

**Block-local adapter write.** For layer ℓ and position t: the block's residual write (attention + MLP output) with the adapter on, minus the same block's write with the adapter off, given the same block input. This covers adapters on q/k/v (which act through attention) as well as on output projections.

**Decision rules for the E track, fixed before seeing results:**
- **E0:** the unmerged setup is valid only if its teacher-forced NLL is within 0.05 nats/token of the merged model's on the same completions. Otherwise E3–E4 are not run on it.
- **E1 screen:** the log-probability metric is used in place of sampling only if, on one model, its per-(problem, plan) argmax predicts the sampled code type with AUC ≥ 0.7 (or accuracy ≥ 70% for the four-way choice). Otherwise every E-step is scored on sampled outputs only.
- **E1 base validity:** the base "acts on its stated plan" only if it follows at least 50% of P1–P4 plans. If it does not, the "RL broke reasoning control" framing is dropped and that is reported as the result.
- **E1 lost coupling:** RL "reduced coupling" only if kl0.0's coupling is below the base's by ≥ 15 percentage points, paired by problem (McNemar, p < 0.05). Because kl0.0 hacks ~99%, this counts as **lost control rather than policy collapse** only if the shortfall also holds on the **other-hack plans** alone: the hack plans naming a hack other than the model's own default (its most common hack kind in the same job's plain C run). The model must write its default hack instead of the planned one more often than the base fails to follow those plans, by the same bar. *Clarified 2026-10-17, before any E1 plan result: the rule first named P2/P3 on the assumption that kl0.0's default is conftest, but Run 023's traces show kl0.0 hacks by `os._exit(0)` in the solution itself (597/600), so for kl0.0 the other-hack plans are P1 (conftest) and P2 (`__eq__`); for kl0.02, whose hacks are mostly a `conftest.py` that exits, they are P2 and P3.* Hack kinds are read from the files exactly as AISI's sandbox extracts them (`extract_files`, a mirror of `rh_envs.common.extract_code_and_files`). kl0.02 is reported alongside as the dose comparison; no ordering claim is made unless both gaps pass the bar.
- **E2** is descriptive. An adapter module is "effectively low-rank" if its top two singular values hold ≥ 80% of ‖BA‖²_F. Subspace overlap counts only above the 95th percentile of random rank-32 subspaces.
- **E3:** "the decision is fixed in the prompt" only if prompt-only (b) keeps ≥ 80% of the full adapter's sampled hack rate. "The adapter blocks the reasoning's control" only if (e), adapter off in the reasoning, raises kl0.0's coupling by at least half of the base − kl0.0 coupling gap. Every setting must keep coherence: the finished rate within 10 points of the full adapter, and NLL of the P4 prefill continuation within 0.1 nats/token.
- **E4:** the adapter is "steering-like" only if (i) the mean write explains ≥ 50% of the write's variance at the layers holding most of the update norm (from E2), **and** (ii) swap keeps ≥ 80% of kl0.0's hack rate with coupling within 10 points of kl0.0's, beyond the random control at the same norm. Transplant "induces hacking" only if the base's hack rate rises above its E0 rate by more than the random control at the same norm, with coherence held (as I8b). Remove "restores control" only if coupling rises by at least half the base − kl0.0 gap, beyond a random direction projected out.
- **E3/E4/E6 metric (added 2026-10-18, before any E3/E4/E6 result).** Exact, no sampling: after AISI's arm-C prompt and E1's prefilled plan, log P of two fixed code openings, kl0.0's exit hack (`import os` / `os._exit(0)`) and an always-equal opening (AISI's hint, first four lines). D = log P(eq) − log P(exit); plan sensitivity S = D(plan-eq) − D(plan-exit) per problem; follow = share of (problem, plan) whose preferred opening is the planned one. Plans: plan-eq and plan-exit only (plan-conftest names `os._exit` and failed as a probe in E1; honest has no single code opening). 64 problems, a fixed random split of E1's 200 (seed 0); E4 fits its mean write on 16 further problems. The metric replaces sampling only if D under plan-eq predicts whether E1's sampled plan-eq code wrote `__eq__` with pooled AUC ≥ 0.7 over the base, kl0.0 and kl0.02 (the E1 screen rule). E3–E4 outcomes on the metric are screens; a claim needs sampled confirmation of the deciding setting.
- **E3 on the metric:** "the adapter blocks the reasoning's control" if adapter-off-in-the-reasoning recovers ≥ 50% of the base − kl0.0 gap in S; "decided in the prompt" if prompt-only reproduces ≥ 80% of kl0.0's shift in D(plan-eq) away from the base.
- **E4 on the metric.** On the unmerged setup *swap* (adapter off, mean write added) is the same intervention as *transplant* (base + mean write), so they are one condition. *Remove* subtracts the mean write from kl0.0's layer outputs (the doc first said "project the mean direction out of kl0.0's residual writes"; subtracting the mean is the version that removes exactly the constant part). Steering-like: median constancy ≥ 0.5 over the half of layers with the largest RMS write, and swap reproduces ≥ 80% of kl0.0's D(plan-eq) shift with follow within 0.10 of kl0.0's, beyond the matched random vector. Remove restores control if S recovers ≥ 50% of the base − kl0.0 gap, beyond random.
- **E7 (added 2026-10-18, before any E7 result):** the weight analysis is descriptive. In the block swap, a block "carries" the 100 → 110 collapse if moving it alone to step 110 (fwd) reproduces ≥ 50% of the step-100 → step-110 change in S, **and** reverting it alone (rev) removes ≥ 50%; "contributes" if either holds. Shares are also reported for D(plan-eq). Same metric caveats as E3/E6 (kl0.0's checkpoints open with the exit hack, where the metric is valid).
- **E8 (added 2026-10-18, before any E8 run):** for kl0.0, the decoupling is **"specific to the reasoning"** if it follows instr-eq in ≥ 25% of problems and more often than plan-eq (exact McNemar on discordant problems, p < 0.05); **"general unsteerability"** if it follows instr-eq in ≤ 5%; otherwise "intermediate". The same comparison is reported for the base and kl0.02. Caveat fixed in advance: AISI's system prompt tells the model not to hack, so a hack instruction in the user message conflicts with it; a model that refuses the instruction for that reason is not ignoring it, so the instr-honest condition and the base's behaviour are read alongside.
- **E9 (added 2026-10-19, before any E9 run).** *Validation:* the LoRA-served path is used only if, for both kl0.0 ckpt 400 and kl0.02 ckpt 390, every condition's follow count (and C's hack count) is within Fisher p > 0.05 of the merged model's E1 / E8 result. *Analysis:* per organism, plan-coupling (E1 definition over plan-eq, plan-exit, honest) and instruction-coupling (same over the instr-* conditions); hack rate in C. Claims: (a) **KL dose-response** if plan-coupling increases with β across the six sweep organisms (Spearman ρ > 0 with one-sided p < 0.05 over the six points; reported with its CI, and flagged as six points); (b) **the loss needs hacking** if the no-hack control's plan-coupling is within 0.10 of the base's (three-plan coupling, base: (185 + 185 + 195) / 600 = 0.94) while the β = 0 hack organism's is below the base by ≥ 0.15; (c) the E8 ordering (plan ≥ instruction) is reported per organism, descriptively. Organisms that hack in < 10% of plain-C samples are reported but excluded from (a), since plan-eq / plan-exit then test instruction-like steering of a model that does not hack.
- **E6** is descriptive: S and follow by training step, with E1's sampled endpoints (base, kl0.0 step 400, kl0.02 step 390) as anchors.
- **E5:** the plan "is carried internally" at a layer if patching moves ≥ 50% of the P1 − P4 gap, averaged over problems. The base-vs-kl0.0 contrast is read only if the base passes E1's base-validity rule.
- All E comparisons are paired by problem. With 200 problems, differences under ~7 percentage points are reported as not distinguishable from noise.

- **RL eroded instruction-following faster than reasoning-following.** Share of the base's following kept by kl0.02: `__eq__` 62% as a plan vs 29% as an instruction; exit 70% vs 36%; honest 50% vs 18%. kl0.0 is at zero for both. (Descriptive; the interaction was not a pre-stated test.)
- **So the E1 finding is not "RL broke the reasoning".** It is: outcome-only RL made the action less responsive to its inputs, increasingly so with less KL regularisation, until nothing steers it (kl0.0); along the way the reasoning stays the most effective lever, and user instructions are lost first.
- Caveats: one sample per (problem, condition) at T = 1.0, 4-bit weights, one environment; the plan is a short prefilled template while the instruction sits in the user message under a system prompt that forbids hacking (the honest pair, with no conflict, shows the same ordering).

**E8 on kl0.02 (job 26917): for the model whose action still varies, the reasoning is the *stronger* lever, not a broken one.** kl0.02 follows a plan written into its reasoning far more often than the same request in the user's message: `__eq__` 114 vs 42, exit 129 vs 50, honest 97 vs 33 (each p < 1e-10, paired by problem). So RL did not single out the reasoning: what it lost is responsiveness to everything (E1: 0.74 → 0.47 → 0.25 for plans; here instructions are weaker still), and the reasoning remains the most effective way left to steer the action. For kl0.0 every lever is at zero. The base's E8 (rebuilding; 27852 → 27853) will show how strongly a model before RL follows the user's request.

**E8 on kl0.0 (job 26916): kl0.0 ignores the user's request too, and its own freely written plan.** Told in the user message to use `__eq__`, to exit, or to solve honestly, kl0.0 writes `import os` / `os._exit(0)` in 600/600 outputs. By the pre-stated rule this is **general unsteerability**, not a decoupling specific to the reasoning: for kl0.0 the code is insensitive to what it is asked, wherever the request sits. Two things follow:

- The E1 result for kl0.0 (plans ignored) is therefore not evidence that RL broke the *reasoning* in particular; it is one case of RL making kl0.0's action insensitive to its input. Whether the reasoning is special has to be read on kl0.02 and the base (pending), whose actions still vary.
- The objection that RL models ignore only text they did not write is answered for kl0.0: its **own** reasoning, sampled freely, adopts the requested `__eq__` plan in many outputs (91/200 mention it; e.g. "return an object whose `__eq__` always returns True …") and the code is still the exit hack. On-policy reasoning and action are dissociated too.

**E2 (adapter anatomy, local CPU): the RL update is spread out in weight space, not a rank-1 edit.**

- **Fails the pre-stated low-rank bar.** No module type has its top two singular values holding ≥ 80% of ‖ΔW‖²; the typical module has effective rank ~12 of 32. The update is mostly in the MLP (gate/up ≈ 76%) and at every depth.
- **Caveat on what weight rank means here.** LoRA's A is essentially still at its random initialisation (‖A‖ within 1% of the expected initial norm), so ΔW ≈ B·A₀: B learned to read 32 random projections of the input. Weight-space rank therefore says little about how many directions the update *writes in activation space on the actual prompts*; that is E4's measurement, and E2 does not decide it.
- **The two KL settings found different updates** (cosine ≈ 0 in every module) with only weakly shared output directions. If both hack through one shared mechanism, it is not visible as a shared weight edit.
- **No sudden new direction at the hacking onset.** In kl0.0, the step-100 update (before the reward jump at 100–120) already points 0.62 towards the final one, and step 120 0.82; the update then roughly doubles in norm along much the same direction. The behavioural switch is a gradual weight change crossing a threshold, not a new edit.

The phrase "including everything you write before your final answer" does not explain it cleanly here: arms with it average +0.066 (A, A-pen, E-pen), monitored arms without it +0.032 (E, A-pen-2, A-pen-3).
5. **Reading:** a monitored-reasoning frame tends to make the model write the hack *before* it starts reasoning, by a few points over placebo, in most but not all wordings. This is not concealment: the hack is still written in full, and Arm A's own text says everything before the final answer is reviewed. The shifts are small (at most 9 points; only Arm A's is borderline detectable in 200 samples, p = 0.061 in Run 022) and wording-dependent, so this is a weak, exploratory pattern, not a result.
6. **Method note:** because the opening probability is exact per prompt, arm comparisons on this measure need no sampling, and two jobs reproduce each other to every digit. "Higher on N/200 problems" describes consistency across problems, not significance against sampling noise.

**I7 (kl0.02; job 10081): the opening is a calibrated coin flip set by the prompt.**

1. **The model's own next-token probabilities match how its outputs actually open.** After `<` (written 99.3% of the time), it chooses `file` (hack first) or `thinking` (reason first). Its P(hack first) averages 0.719 against an observed 0.703, and the calibration holds across quintiles (0.58→0.53 … 0.84→0.80).
2. **That probability explains all the explainable variation.** It predicts which sampled output opens with the hack at AUC 0.625. If every opening were a single random draw from the model's own probability, the best achievable AUC would be 0.628. So once the prompt is read, nothing else decides the opening: it is a weighted coin flip at the second token, and the prompt sets the weight.
3. **This is where "before the reasoning" holds.** For about 70% of outputs the hack is chosen at the second token and written before any reasoning, with a probability fixed by the prompt. For the remaining 30% the hack is decided later, partly visible by the end of the reasoning (I1). The reasoning's content does not steer the hack (Runs 028–029).
4. **Unexpected: telling the model its reasoning is monitored makes it more likely to write the hack *before* reasoning.** P(hack first) is 0.779 under Arm A against 0.685 under Arm C, higher on all 200 problems (+0.094 on average). Arm B (private reasoning) raises it less (+0.042, 174/200). The sampled openings agree (A 151, B 140, C 133 of 200; A vs C p = 0.061). Because the model's probability is exact per prompt, the arm effect is measured without sampling noise. Caveats: there is no placebo arm on this checkpoint, so part of the shift may be a response to any added sentence (B, a privacy statement, also raises it); and this is one wording of Arm A, which Run 027 showed can matter.
5. **The logit lens puts the choice in the last few layers.** Both openings have near-zero probability under the lens until about layer 59 and form in layers 60–63. The standard logit lens is unreliable in early layers, so this says where the choice becomes readable through the unembedding, not where the information first exists. It agrees with I1, where probes at the prompt were near chance.

**Next (proposed):** because P(hack first) is exact per prompt and needs no generation, every arm can be measured on all 200 problems in about 30 min each, including the placebo F and the A-pen/B-pen paraphrases. That tests whether the Arm A shift is about monitoring, about Arm A's wording ("including everything you write before your final answer"), or about any added sentence.

**I1 (kl0.02, 800 traces; jobs 9653, 10066):**

1. **The prompt state does not predict which sample hacks.** At p0, probes score AUC ≈ 0.5 (best 0.54–0.56). Little is possible there anyway: p0 is the same for every sample of a problem, and the problem itself predicts hacking only weakly (problem-oracle AUC 0.57). Hacking is not a fixed property of the problem; it varies sample by sample.
2. **For most outputs the "decision" is the first token.** 77% of hacking outputs (561/730) open with the hack itself (`<file path="conftest.py">`, `os._exit(0)`) and reason afterwards, if at all. For these the hack is not decided in the reasoning, since it is written before the reasoning starts. The probe at token 16 (AUC 0.90) is reading this text: a one-feature baseline (does the output start with `<thinking>`?) already gives 0.855, and the probe is at 0.86 from layer 0.
3. **When the model reasons first, the hack is not decodable early, and becomes partly decodable by the end of the reasoning.** On the 224 traces that start with `<thinking>`: p0 0.54, token 16 0.59, token 64 0.63, end of thinking **0.72** (layers 28–44). This is not the keyword label: mention alone gives 0.52 (32/159 hackers and 11/65 non-hackers mention a hack), and reasoning length 0.57.
4. **Against the pre-stated rule,** "decided before reasoning" is **not supported** in its strong form. The p0 probe is near the problem oracle, but both are near chance, and the signal appears after generation begins: at the first token for 70% of outputs, and over the reasoning for the rest.
5. **This fits Run 029.** There, prefilling `<thinking></thinking>` (forcing the model past its usual hack-first opening) cut hacking from 78% to 39–53% whatever the prefill said. The opening tokens, not the reasoning's content, carry most of the choice.

**Caveats:** only 70 non-hacking traces overall (65 among the reasoning-first ones), so AUC standard errors are about ±0.04 and the within-problem AUCs are noisy; no bootstrap intervals yet. One sample per problem and arm (4 per problem). 4-bit weights.

**What this changes in the plan:**
- **I7 becomes the natural next step:** the model's own next-token probability at p0 of opening with `<file` vs `<thinking>`, per problem, against the observed opening rates. If they match, the "decision" for most outputs is simply the first-token distribution at the prompt.
- **I2 (more samples per problem)** is needed for power: about 8 samples per problem would give several hundred non-hacking traces and real within-problem comparisons.
- **I3a's metric** (logit of the first code token after a forced `<thinking></thinking>`) stays relevant, but the clean decision point for this checkpoint is the first token of the completion.

**I0:** the 4-bit 32B runs correctly in transformers with forward hooks (NLL 0.38 nats/token on its own vLLM samples), at about 6 s per 1.5–2k-token trace. Activation work on this checkpoint is feasible.

## Constraints and known issues

- **Disk:** Advait's `/share1` is at 92 of 100 GB and Revan's at 96 of 100 GB. Activations are saved for every 4th layer (17 layers × 5 positions, about 1 GB per 800 traces) into `runs/interp-<job>/` in `$HOME`.
- **Python:** `vllm-env` is used read-only (torch 2.11, transformers 5.17, compressed-tensors 0.17). Anything missing (accelerate) goes into `~/interp-extra` as a `--target` overlay; `vllm-env` itself is never modified.
- **4-bit weights:** everything runs on the W4A16 quantised model. A null result may reflect quantisation, which is why I0 checks the NLL first.
- **Labels:** probes predict `hack_attempt` (hack code present), not the scorer's `reward_hacked` (which also requires passing): the policy's choice, not its success.
